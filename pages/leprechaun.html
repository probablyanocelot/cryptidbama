“Crichton Leprechaun” AKA “Mobile Leprechaun” or “Alabama Leprechaun” Location: Mobile, Alabama (LeCren Street in Crichton, a neighborhood in Mobile, AL)


Lore: It was March 2006 on LeCren Street when things started getting strange. Under the heavy shadows near UMS-Wright, a crowd began to gather, pulled in by whispers of something that shouldn’t exist: a leprechaun, tucked away in the branches of a tree. Shun Thomas claims to be the first to see the figure emerging just beyond the leaves. According to his own account, he was halfway through his 1st beer when he caught a glimpse of something that didn’t quite make sense. He did a double-take, then another, but the image remained fixed in place. He hesitantly pointed the apparition out to his brother, cousin, and a friend, checking with them to confirm that they too could see the creature hidden in the shadows of the greenery. The men all watched in disbelief as a metamorphosis took place before their eyes. A face began to pull itself out of the mist, first a chin, then a mouth and nose, all the way up to the brim of a top hat. Plenty of people in Crichton claim they saw it, but the creature remained camera-shy; disappearing every time someone attempted to capture an image on tape or even shine a light in its direction. It only existed in the mysteriousness of the dark, leaving the locals as the only people to ever get a chance to experience it for themselves. The leprechaun would return every evening for a week, that is, until news crews and cameras caught wind of the enigma.

Eventually, the world would turn the story into a punchline, but you can still hear the frustration in Thomas’s voice – the sound of a man who saw something magical, but society continues to tell him it’s not possible. While his sister’s famous sketch went viral and eventually sold on eBay for $1,100 to New York resident, Tye Miksis, with proceeds benefiting the American Cancer Society’s Relay for Life, Thomas created his own record of the account – a painting that captures what the cameras couldn’t. His advice for anyone looking into the shadows? Don’t take someone else’s word for it. Seek out the truth for yourself, ask your own questions, and decide what you’re really seeing when the lights go out.
