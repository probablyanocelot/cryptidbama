White Thang

“White Thang” AKA “the Downey Booger” or “Winston Wildman” Location: Bankhead National Forest/Double Springs, Alabama

Said to be native to northern Alabama, specifically the north-central counties in Alabama (including but not limited to: Winston, Cullman, Morgan, Lawrence, Marshall, Madison, Limestone and Lauderdale Counties). The Appalachian Mountains stretch nearly 2000 miles from Alabama up into Canada and have long been steeped in Cryptid lore. Among the most enduring tales is that of Bigfoot. Sightings in the region date back centuries with the Cherokee and other indigenous traditions speaking of giant, hairy men who live deep in the mountains. In the 20th century, reports of towering figures, oversized footprints, and unearthly howls only cemented the Appalachians as one of the creatures prime stopping grounds.

Lore: Since the late 19th century, a pale shadow known as “White Thang” has haunted the jagged landscapes of Winston County, Alabama. This narrative is said to have originated on a humid evening when cousins, John & Joe Downey, were navigating a narrow, moonlit path home from a social gathering. When, without warning, a towering, spectral figure suddenly appeared causing their horses to rear back in blind terror. The mounts, trembling and wide-eyed, refused to step another inch toward the beast, forcing the boys to navigate their way through a lengthy detour, in the darkness only an Alabama forest and night sky could conjure. When they finally stumbled home at dawn, breathless and utterly exhausted, their family met their tale with cold skepticism, dismissing the “apparition” as a frantic lie to cover a night of youthful abandon. The credibility of the phenomenon only increased when, just a few short months later a family travelling to church down the same narrow path would find it blocked by the same hulking, alabaster figure. And just like the Downey boys before them, the family could only watch as the being vanished back into the densely packed woods from which it had just emerged, at an impossible, fluid speed. The most chilling account, however, belongs to a traveler named Jim Jackson. While driving his wagon, loaded to the brim with homemade moonshine, through the same suffocating stretch of woods a prickling sensation crawled up his neck-the unmistakable weight of a predatory gaze. Looking back into the shifting shadows, he spotted a bizarre, shaggy-white beast pursuing him on two legs, its form blurred by the low-hanging mist. In the chaos of the encounter, Jackson discharged his firearm and a piercing guttural shriek erupted-a harrowing fusion of a human wail and a primal howl that seemed to vibrate through the very marrow of his bones. Then, an absolute heavy silence swallowed the woods. When the area was later examined, no creature was found-only flattened ferns and few dark mysterious droplets staining the forest floor remained as the sole evidence of the confrontation. To this day, local whispers describe the entity as a towering, seven-to-eight-foot figure covered in matted, snow-white hair that seems to glow with an otherworldly luminescence against the deepest greens of the Alabama wilderness.
